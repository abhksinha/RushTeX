\section{The enumerate package}
The \texttt{enumerate} package leaves a list without an optional argument as the kernel
makes it. With one, such as \texttt{[(i)]}, each \texttt{A}, \texttt{a}, \texttt{i},
\texttt{I} or \texttt{1} outside braces stands for the counter in that style, the rest is
copied, and the left margin of that depth becomes the width of the label at seven plus
\texttt{labelsep}:
\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
  \item a first item in lower-case roman numerals,\label{it:tmpl}
  \item a second, holding a list of its own:
    \begin{enumerate}[{Case} A:]
      \item the word in braces is copied, the letter counts,\label{it:case}
      \item and the margin fits \emph{Case G:}.
    \end{enumerate}
\end{enumerate}
The new counter form reaches the references too: items~\ref{it:tmpl} and~\ref{it:case}.
